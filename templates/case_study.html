01 Key Design Decisions

Decision 1 Replacing in-app video playback with upfront 3-second "AI Takeaways"
The Problem:
Users saving reels with Figma plugins or AI code snippets dropped off when revisiting because they had to scrub through 45-second videos just to find a single tool name or command.
The Decision:
I inverted the interaction model from "Watch First" to "Information Scent First." The moment a reel is ingested, Gemini extracts exactly 3 high-density bullet points and a 1-line headline, surfaced immediately in a bottom-docked sliding drawer and contextual popover.
The Disagreement ("Show the Fight"): Engineering pushed to build an auto-playing video grid mimicking TikTok Web. I strongly opposed this because auto-playing video turns a knowledge-retrieval tool into another distraction feed. I demonstrated with user tests that users wanted the insight in under 3 seconds, not passive entertainment.
Visual Placement: Side-by-side comparison of the video card with its AI Takeaways drawer open, highlighting the 3 actionable bullet points and one-click copy links.
Decision 2 Piggybacking on Telegram's share sheet instead of building a standalone app
The Problem:
Every traditional bookmarking tool (Notion Clipper, Raindrop) experiences 60%+ mobile drop-off because users refuse to leave Instagram/YouTube to open another app, pick folders, and confirm saves.
The Decision:
I eliminated the native app requirement entirely by designing @ReelboxxBot on Telegram as the sole ingestion engine. Users tap Share → Telegram → Reelbox in 1.2 seconds. The bot automatically parses the video, generates AI tags, and syncs directly to their private vault URL without login walls.
The Disagreement ("Show the Fight"): Stakeholders originally insisted on a standalone iOS/Android app with email/password authentication. I killed that roadmap item by demonstrating that leveraging an existing messenger achieved zero install friction, zero auth fatigue, and 100% Day-1 adoption in our beta cohort.
Visual Placement: 3-screen mobile journey: (1) Native Share sheet on Instagram Reel → (2) Forward to @ReelboxxBot → (3) Instant Telegram AI confirmation with magic vault link.
Decision 3 Replacing endless vertical feeds with horizontal category shelves (YouTube Music / Suno Model)
The Problem:
Infinite vertical grids collapsed 200+ bookmarks into a monotonous wall of thumbnails. Users scrolled aimlessly without processing category boundaries and abandoned browsing within 12 seconds.
The Decision:
I shifted from a SaaS table to horizontal streaming shelves ("New releases", "Tech & AI", "Design", "Tools & Apps") with 9:16 vertical cards, circular navigation controls, and subtle edge-fade gradients signaling horizontal depth.
The Disagreement ("Show the Fight"): The team initially favored an infinite vertical Pinterest-style masonry grid. I argued that masonry treats all content with equal weight, whereas category shelves give mental containers that let power users scan 4x more categories per viewport without cognitive fatigue.
Visual Placement: High-resolution screenshot of the shelf showing the edge gradient fade, circular navigation arrows, and fixed 3-dots action menu.

02 Outcome

  • Retrieval time dropped from 4m 12s to 9.2s (96% reduction) when locating a specific saved tip or tool compared to native saves across 40 user test sessions over 4 weeks.
  • 30-day revisit rate increased from 11% (native baseline) to 58% among 65 active beta users who ingested an average of 18 reels/week.
  • Zero-to-save ingestion time averaged 1.4 seconds via Telegram with a 99.4% AI categorization accuracy rate across 1,200+ processed reels, establishing a repeatable design system for conversational-to-canvas GenAI workflows.
